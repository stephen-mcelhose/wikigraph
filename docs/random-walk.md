# Random Walk on a Wiki

A **random walk** on a wiki is a sequence of page visits generated by
following outgoing `[[wikilinks]]` uniformly at random. At each step the
walker picks one of the current page's outgoing links with equal probability
and moves there. The process repeats indefinitely.

This model sits at the heart of every metric wikigraph computes. See
[[markov-model]] for how the transition matrix is built from wikilinks.

## Definition

Let G be a directed graph where nodes are pages and edges are wikilinks.
At time t the walker is on page i. At time t+1 it moves to page j with
probability:

```
Pᵢⱼ = 1 / out-degree(i)   if i links to j
     = 0                   otherwise
```

P is the **row-stochastic transition matrix** — every row sums to 1.
[[sink-page|Sink pages]] (zero out-degree) are handled by uniform
teleportation so the chain stays well-defined.

## Connection to the transition matrix

The transition matrix P encodes one-step probabilities completely. A two-step
walk from i to k has probability Σⱼ Pᵢⱼ Pⱼₖ — the (i,k) entry of P².
In general the n-step transition probability is the (i,j) entry of Pⁿ.

`catrace.NewRandomWalkKernel(adj)` builds P from a raw adjacency matrix by
row-normalising. See [[catrace]] for the full API.

## Long-run behaviour — the stationary distribution

As n → ∞, Pⁿ converges (within each [[communicating-classes|communicating class]]) to a matrix
whose rows are all equal to π — the **[[stationary-distribution]]**. π(i) is
the fraction of time the walk spends on page i in the long run. High-π pages
are structural hubs; low-π pages are peripheral.

## Why it models a wiki reader

A random walk approximates a reader who follows links without topic preference.
This is a reasonable null model: it captures the link topology's influence on
navigation without assuming any particular intent. Pages visited often under
this null model are genuinely well-connected hubs, not just frequently written
about.

## Relationship to PageRank

Google's PageRank adds a **damping factor** d: with probability d the walker
follows a link; with probability 1−d it teleports to a uniformly random page.
wikigraph's model is similar but more targeted — teleportation applies only to
[[sink-page|sink pages]] rather than at every step. The effect is the same:
the chain stays ergodic and a unique [[stationary-distribution|π]] exists.

## What wikigraph computes from the walk

| Quantity                       | What it measures                                           |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[stationary-distribution]]    | Long-run visit frequency per page; used as centrality      |
| [[mfpt]]                       | Expected steps from page i to first reach page j           |
| [[commute-time]]               | MFPT(i,j) + MFPT(j,i) — symmetric distance metric         |
| [[entropy-rate]]               | Bits of randomness produced per step                       |
| [[communicating-classes]]      | Which pages are mutually reachable by the walk             |

All of these are exposed via the [[analyze]] subcommand and computed by the
[[catrace]] library.
